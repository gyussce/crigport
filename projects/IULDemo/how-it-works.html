How Indexed Universal Life Works

The plain-English version. No jargon walls, no sales tricks — just how the mechanics actually work.

The basics

IUL in 60 Seconds

Indexed Universal Life is a type of permanent life insurance. You pay premiums; part covers the death benefit, and the rest goes into a cash value account. That cash value grows based on the performance of a market index (like the S&P 500) — but with two guardrails.

▲

The Cap

Your gains are capped — typically between 9% and 12% depending on the carrier and index. If the S&P returns 22% in a year, you'd get credited the cap amount. You give up the top in exchange for protection at the bottom.

▬

The Floor

Your losses are floored at 0%. If the S&P drops 35%, your cash value stays flat — you don't gain anything, but you don't lose anything either. This is the core trade-off that makes IUL different from direct market investing.

Step by step

Where Your Money Goes

1

You pay a premium

Monthly or annually — the amount is flexible. You can pay more in good years and less in lean ones, as long as the policy stays funded. This isn't like term insurance where the premium is fixed.

2

The carrier deducts the cost of insurance

A portion pays for the death benefit and administrative costs. This is the "insurance" part. The cost goes up as you age, which is why overfunding early matters — more of your dollar goes to cash value when you're younger.

3

The rest goes into your cash value

This is the accumulation account. You choose how it's credited — tied to an index like the S&P 500, a fixed rate, or a blend. You're not actually invested in the market; the carrier uses options strategies to mirror index performance within the cap and floor.

4

Cash value grows — and you can access it

Once you've built up enough cash value (typically 10–15 years of funding), you can take policy loans against it. These loans are not taxable income. You're essentially borrowing from yourself, and the loan balance is settled by the death benefit when you pass.

Honest take

IUL Isn't For Everyone

I turn away about half the people who call me. Here's how to know if it fits.

It might be a good fit if you...

  • ✓ Already max out your 401(k) or IRA
  • ✓ Have 15+ years before you need the money
  • ✓ Want tax diversification in retirement
  • ✓ Need a death benefit anyway
  • ✓ Can commit to $300–$1,000+/month

It's probably not right if you...

  • ✗ Haven't maxed your employer match yet
  • ✗ Carry high-interest debt (credit cards, etc.)
  • ✗ Need the money within 10 years
  • ✗ Want maximum market exposure
  • ✗ Don't need life insurance
Common questions

Things People Ask Me

I get this question a lot, and honestly, I get why. IUL has been oversold by some agents who use it like a magic bullet. It's not. It's a legitimate insurance product issued by regulated carriers like Pacific Life and National Life Group. The problem isn't the product — it's agents who show only the best-case scenario and gloss over the costs. I show you the good, the bad, and the "here's what happens if the market is flat for five years."

IUL premiums are flexible — that's one of the advantages. If you have a tight month, you can reduce or skip a payment as long as there's enough cash value to cover the cost of insurance. That said, consistently underfunding the policy will hurt long-term performance. I design policies with a cushion so one bad quarter doesn't derail things.

Both give you tax-free income in retirement, but they work differently. A Roth IRA has income limits and contribution caps ($7,000/year in 2025). IUL has no income limits and no IRS contribution cap (though MEC rules apply). A Roth gives you direct market access; IUL gives you index-linked growth with a floor. Ideally, you'd have both. They complement each other well.

IUL does have internal costs — cost of insurance, administrative charges, and premium loads. In the early years, a meaningful chunk of your premium goes to these charges. That's why IUL is a long-term tool, not a short-term play. I'll show you a full breakdown of every charge in your illustration. No hidden fees. If the numbers don't work for your situation, I'll tell you.

I'm independent, so I'm not captive to one company. I primarily work with National Life Group, Pacific Life, North American (Sammons Financial), and Nationwide. Each has different strengths — cap rates, loan provisions, riders. I match the carrier to your needs, not the other way around.
